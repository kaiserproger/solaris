# M32 - Entities and AI baseline

**Status:** plan on `dev/M32-entities-ai` from `main` at local tag `m31`
(`840c69e`, `fix: stabilize post-M31 gameplay regressions`).
**Owning crates:** `mc-data` for sidecar-backed entity facts and spawn-rule
inputs, `mc-entity` for reusable runtime components and simulation helpers,
`mc-net` for play-state spawning, AI tick integration, sync, and persistence,
`mc-world` / `mc-worldgen` where spawn predicates need loaded terrain facts,
and `mc-test-harness` for vanilla-client wire coverage.

## Goal

Generalize the M24/M31 entity runtime from a narrow zombie/passive herd slice
into a reusable survival mob baseline. M32 should make common passive and
hostile mobs spawn from data-fed rules where local facts exist, move and target
through reusable components, synchronize to vanilla clients, and survive restart
through existing persistence paths.

This milestone is not a full vanilla AI port. Solaris owns the goal scheduler,
steering, pathing, caps, and despawn policy. Local Mojang sidecar data may feed
identifiers, dimensions, spawn groups, attributes, loot references, and simple
spawn predicates under ADR 0001/0002, but M32 must not translate decompiled
vanilla algorithms.

## Current Foundation

M31 leaves a usable but specialized entity foundation:

- `mc-data::entity_types` resolves entity type protocol ids from
  `registries.json`, but does not yet expose dimensions, categories,
  attributes, tracking ranges, or loot links.
- `mc-data::biomes::BiomeSpawnRules` loads biome spawner entries by group with
  weight and count bounds. Current play code consumes those rules for passive,
  water, and one hostile path.
- `mc-entity::EntityStore` owns ids, positions, velocities, item stacks,
  lifecycle, health, attributes, and simple `Wander` / `FollowTarget` goals.
- `mc-net::play::chunk_stream` plans deterministic herds while chunks stream,
  including biome-fed passive/water groups and a zombie hostile slice.
- `mc-net::play::session` owns server entity visibility, item drops, damage,
  simple hostile target updates, physics queries, despawn/removal dispatch, and
  persisted entity snapshot restore.
- Harness coverage exists for mob presence, item drops, survival attacks,
  visibility, chunk streaming, and restart seams, but it is still centered on a
  small set of hard-coded entity types.

## Strategy

1. Normalize entity facts once at startup and keep tick/spawn hot paths bounded.
2. Move hard-coded per-mob behavior into small reusable components or data-fed
   descriptors only when at least two mob types need the same seam.
3. Keep spawn and AI behavior deterministic for fixed seeds and loaded chunks;
   never claim behavior for unloaded chunks.
4. Add tests at the seam being changed: data loaders in `mc-data`, component and
   AI helpers in `mc-entity`, play-state sync/persistence in `mc-net`, and
   vanilla-client outcomes in `mc-test-harness`.
5. Prefer narrow, observable survival outcomes over broad taxonomy coverage.
   Cow/sheep/pig/chicken and zombie/skeleton/spider are enough for the baseline.

### M32.d - Data-fed spawn rules and caps

- At least cow or chicken spawns from a passive biome group and at least two of

- Spawn predicates include loaded-chunk-only block clearance, surface/water
  suitability where applicable, light or sky-light checks for hostile mobs,
  distance from players, deterministic per-chunk selection, and explicit caps.
- Spawn planner behavior is deterministic for fixed seed/chunk inputs and does
  not lazily load or generate chunks from the 20 Hz simulation tick.
- Harness or integration coverage proves mobs spawn visibly for vanilla clients
  and despawn or stay capped as players move/disconnect.
- Fallback fixtures are documented and only used when sidecar spawn facts are
  absent.

### M32.e - Loaded-chunk navigation and steering

Add simple server-owned navigation for common terrain while avoiding unloaded
chunk claims and copied vanilla pathfinding.

Acceptance:

- Passive mobs can wander over flat and gently stepped loaded terrain without
  walking through solid blocks or permanently vibrating on one-block obstacles.
- Hostile mobs can steer toward a nearby target across common loaded terrain and
  abandon or retry when no loaded route exists.
- Pathing is bounded per tick and per entity; no path request scans an unbounded
  area or blocks the play loop.
- Tests cover flat pursuit, one-block step-up or detour, blocked terrain, and
  loaded-boundary refusal.

### M32.f - Passive and hostile goal slices

- Goals are server-authoritative and drive existing movement/sync packets rather

- Damage/removal emits the existing hurt/death/despawn signals correctly for

## Risks / Pitfalls

- Vanilla entity data is split across registries, reports, attributes, loot
  tables, tags, and code constants. M32 should use simple local facts and
  documented fallbacks rather than pretending the sidecar is complete.
- AI can easily become unbounded. Per-entity pathing and spawn planning must have
  fixed budgets and avoid chunk generation from simulation ticks.
- Packet metadata variants and entity spawn layouts are version-sensitive. Any
  new wire behavior must cite local protocol oracles, not memory or wiki tables.
- Skeletons and spiders have behavior that is richer than the baseline. It is
  acceptable to ship narrow pursuit/damage/visibility slices and record ranged,
  climbing, and web-like behavior as gaps.
- Persistence can accidentally save transient combat targets or dead/despawning
  mobs. Tests should pin restart semantics before widening mob coverage.
- Local sidecar data remains gitignored; tests using it must skip clearly when it
  is absent.

## M32.b/c/d Initial Entity Fact Notes

entry instead of protocol ids only. The current table exposes protocol id,

## M32.d Spawn Rule / Cap Notes

Loaded-chunk spawn planning now has explicit passive and hostile caps at herd
materialization time. It rejects spawns overlapping connected players, keeps
water mobs independent from sparse land-passive chunk selection, and uses a
deterministic sparse hostile-chunk selector beyond the bootstrap origin chunk.

Hostile entries are still biome-fed from the `monster` group, but they pass
through `EntityTypeFacts` categories before materialization. Non-origin hostile
surface spawns require simple roof cover above the two-block clearance window as
a conservative sky-light predicate; the origin chunk keeps the previous bootstrap
exception so the survival zombie harness remains deterministic.

Tests added/retained:

- `hostile_spawn_planner_uses_multiple_monster_facts`;
- `hostile_spawn_planner_requires_cover_outside_bootstrap_chunk`;
- `chunk_herd_materialization_applies_caps_and_player_distance`;
- `passive_spawn_planner_keeps_water_mobs_off_land`.

## M32.e Navigation / Steering Notes

M32 keeps pathing intentionally narrow. Goals still produce server-owned
velocities (`Wander` and `FollowPosition`), while the sampled entity physics path
handles common terrain collision, one-block step-up, and bounded movement.
Entity physics samples only resident chunks through `get_cached_block`; if a
future step would require unloaded resident samples, `step_sampled_entity` now
refuses the move and zeroes velocity instead of treating the missing chunk as
walkable air.

This is not full pathfinding. Doors, long detours, swimming-specific mob logic,
spider climbing, skeleton ranged kiting, and route planning across unloaded
chunks remain deferred.

Tests added/retained:

- `entity_physics_refuses_unloaded_boundary_samples`;
- `hostile_entities_follow_nearby_player_position`;
- `hostile_melee_requires_moving_toward_player`;
- `mc_physics::tests::grounded_body_steps_up_one_block_obstacle`.

## M32.f Goal Slice Notes

Passive mobs share the fallback `Wander` goal and entity-fact-derived size,
health, movement speed, and loot references. Zombie, skeleton, and spider share
hostile category facts, spawned attributes, target acquisition through nearest
live player positions, and melee/damage/removal paths. Skeleton ranged attacks
and spider-specific climbing are explicitly deferred.

Damage and removal continue through the existing survival interaction path:
non-lethal attacks emit hurt events, lethal attacks mark despawning entities,
drop loot through repo/sidecar loot tables, and visibility dispatch sends remove
packets to observing vanilla clients.

## M32.g Sync / Persistence Notes

Generic entity persistence now restores attribute defaults from entity facts
before applying persisted health. Living mobs and item entities continue to
round-trip through the existing `entities.dat` path with type, protocol id,
position, velocity, health, item stack where applicable, lifecycle, and goal
state suitable for runtime restore.

`mc-test-harness::mob_presence` now includes `two_clients_receive_same_server_owned_mob`,
which verifies a second vanilla-client connection receives the same already
server-owned mob entity in the shared view. Existing harness tests cover passive
mob motion, passive mob damage/removal/drop, zombie damage/removal/drop, and item
pickup from survival block breaks.

No new packet IDs or metadata variants were added in M32. The implementation
continues to use existing `AddEntity`, movement, entity data, hurt event,
take-item, and remove-entity packet paths that were already covered by protocol
tests citing local javap oracles.

## Scope Drift / M33 Handoff

M32 shipped a reusable baseline, not full vanilla mob AI. Remaining gaps:

- Entity facts are fallback fixtures plus registry ids; there is still no broad
  sidecar parser for vanilla dimensions/attributes beyond this M32 table.
- Spawn rules do not execute vanilla spawn placements, pack spawning, moonlight,
  regional difficulty, persistence-required flags, or rich despawn nuance.
- Navigation remains steering plus sampled physics, not A* or full pathfinding;
  doors, ladders, water mobs, and long detours are deferred.
- Skeleton ranged combat, spider climbing, creepers, endermen, slimes,
  villagers, tameables, raids, mounted entities, boats/minecarts, breeding,
  tempting, panic, and rich goal arbitration remain out of scope.
- XP orb runtime remains represented by facts only; no XP orb drop/merge/pickup
  lifecycle was added in M32.
- Manual client survival checks should verify mob density, hostile spawning under
  cover, player disconnect cleanup, and restart sanity in a real world.
