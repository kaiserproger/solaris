# M33 - Combat, effects, and XP

**Status:** plan on `dev/M33-combat-effects-xp` from `main` at local tag
`m32` (`4561963`, `feat: M32 entities and AI baseline`).
**Owning crates:** `mc-data` for damage, item, armor, status-effect, and loot
facts; `mc-entity` for reusable combat/effect/XP runtime state; `mc-net` for
play-state damage hooks, packet sync, durability, drops, and pickups; `mc-world`
where environmental damage needs loaded block/fluid facts; and
`mc-test-harness` for vanilla-client survival coverage.

## Goal

Make survival combat broadly usable after the M32 entity baseline. M33 should
cover player-vs-mob and mob-vs-player melee, at least one non-melee damage path,
data-fed damage/item/armor facts where available, status effects, entity loot,
and XP orb lifecycle enough for vanilla clients to observe normal combat rewards.

This milestone is not a full vanilla combat port. Solaris owns its damage math,
cooldowns, knockback, effect ticking, durability policy, and XP curve. Local
Mojang sidecar data and javap protocol dumps may provide identifiers, packet
layouts, registries, component facts, and record field order under ADR 0001/0002,
but M33 must not translate decompiled gameplay algorithms.

## Current Foundation

M32 leaves a reusable entity and AI baseline:

- `mc-data::entity_types` exposes fallback facts for common passive and hostile
  mobs, items, and experience orbs, including protocol ids, category, dimensions,
  attributes, and loot refs where Solaris has fixtures.
- `mc-entity::EntityStore` owns ids, positions, velocities, item stacks,
  lifecycle, health, attributes, goals, and persisted entity snapshots.
- `mc-net::play::session` already handles survival interaction reach checks,
  simple hostile melee, hurt/death/removal signals, item drops, block drops,
  entity visibility, item pickup, and persistence restore.
- `mc-world` and play physics can sample loaded blocks and resident chunks, but
  environmental damage should avoid forcing chunk generation from simulation
  ticks.
- Harness coverage exists for mob visibility, passive/hostile damage and drops,
  item pickup, chunk streaming, and restart seams, but XP, effects, durability,
  projectiles, and most damage sources are still gaps.

## Strategy

1. Add data seams before gameplay math, with explicit Solaris fallbacks for fresh
   clones and local-sidecar skips where real data is absent.
2. Keep combat deterministic and bounded per tick. No damage or effect path may
   scan unloaded chunks or block the play loop.
3. Prefer narrow observable survival outcomes over broad taxonomy coverage.
   Zombie/skeleton/spider plus cow/chicken or similar common mobs are enough for
   baseline combat and rewards.
4. Verify every new packet id, packet field layout, and entity metadata variant
   from `.analysis/protocol-dump.txt` or `wire-probe` before implementation.
5. Document independent approximations for cooldowns, armor, effects, XP, and
   projectile behavior instead of claiming vanilla parity.

### M33.c - Combat timing and melee rules

Make melee combat reliable and bounded for players and common hostile mobs.

Acceptance:

- Player attacks use server-side reach, line-of-sight or obstruction validation
  where loaded samples are available, attack cooldown, target invulnerability
  frames, hurt cooldown, and knockback.
- Hostile melee uses the same damage pipeline as player attacks with mob attack
  damage from attributes or fallback facts.
- Dead or despawning entities cannot be damaged twice for duplicate drops/XP.
- Tests cover cooldown enforcement, i-frame suppression, knockback direction,
  reach/loaded obstruction rejection, and lethal melee cleanup.

### M33.d - Damage sources and environmental hooks

Route non-melee damage through shared damage-source records while keeping source
coverage intentionally narrow.

Acceptance:

- Fall, drowning, fire/lava, suffocation, starvation, mob melee, player melee,
  projectile, and explosion placeholders or hooks are represented by explicit
  damage sources.
- Implemented environmental damage only uses loaded block/fluid/body state and
  never generates chunks from a damage tick.
- Player death and mob death paths receive enough source context for drops, XP,
  messages or future message hooks, and testing.
- Tests cover at least one non-melee damage path in addition to melee.

### M33.e - Weapons and armor durability

Apply item durability and armor mitigation through the shared damage pipeline.

Acceptance:

- Weapon/tool durability decreases on successful attacks where the held item has
  durability facts.
- Armor mitigation uses data-fed armor facts or documented Solaris fallbacks and
  decreases armor durability on incoming damage.
- Broken weapons, tools, and armor are removed from the correct inventory slot and
  synchronized to vanilla clients.
- Tests cover weapon damage, armor damage reduction, armor durability loss, and
  armor break/removal.

### M33.f - Projectiles

Acceptance:

- Bow/arrow or skeleton-arrow attacks create server-owned projectile entities with

- Projectile hits route through damage sources, i-frames, knockback, drops, and XP

- Harness or integration tests cover visible projectile spawn/motion and a hit or

### M33.g - Status effects

## Risks / Pitfalls

- Damage and item facts are split across registries, reports, item components,
  tags, entity attributes, loot tables, and code constants. M33 should expose
  narrow facts and explicit fallbacks rather than pretending the sidecar is
  complete.
- Combat can become nondeterministic if multiple systems damage or remove the
  same entity in one tick. Death/drop/XP emission must be idempotent.
- Packet metadata for effects, projectiles, XP, equipment, and inventory updates
  is version-sensitive. New wire behavior must cite local protocol oracles.
- Environmental damage can accidentally generate or inspect unloaded chunks. Use
  resident loaded samples only and fail closed where state is unknown.
- XP orbs can create entity-count pressure. Spawning, merging, pickup, and
  despawn must stay bounded.
- Full vanilla combat has many exceptions. Enchantments, potion brewing, shields,
  sweeping, crits, explosions, firework damage, thorns, and boss mechanics are
  likely closeout gaps unless explicitly pulled into scope.

## M33.b Damage / Item Data Notes

`mc-data::damage_types` now exposes a small `DamageTypeTable` keyed by
`Identifier`, with normalized `DamageTypeFacts` for message id, scaling,
exhaustion, effect marker, death-message type, and Solaris damage category. The
loader consumes `data/minecraft/damage_type/*.json` from the local sidecar, and a
separate fallback table covers the M33 baseline sources: generic, player attack,
mob attack, arrow projectile, fall, lava/fire, drowning, suffocation, starvation,
and explosion.

`mc-data::item_components` now preserves combat facts from item component
reports: weapon marker, max damage, attack damage/speed add-value modifiers,
equippable slot, and armor/toughness modifiers. Existing food/tool/stack facts
remain unchanged.

## M33.c Combat Timing Notes

The existing player attack cooldown remains in the play interaction path and now
starts when the server accepts an attack attempt, even if the target is inside
its hurt window. `SessionRegistry::damage_server_entity` now tracks per-entity
hurt invulnerability in world ticks, so repeated melee or future damage-source
hooks cannot damage the same living entity every packet/tick or emit duplicate
death drops before the i-frame window expires. Removing an entity clears the
cooldown entry.

Melee attack damage now prefers `ItemFactsTable` attack-damage modifiers when the
held item has component facts, falling back to the older Solaris item-family
rules otherwise. This keeps fresh clones working while real sidecar reports drive
common weapon damage where available.

This is a narrow timing slice. Rich line-of-sight obstruction, knockback tuning,
and data-fed weapon cooldown math remain for later M33.c/e work.

## M33.e Weapon Durability Notes

Successful melee attacks now damage held weapons/tools with `max_damage` and
`weapon.item_damage_per_attack` from item component facts when present, falling
back to the existing Solaris durability families for tools. Slot updates are sent
through the existing inventory mutation path, and broken held items are removed
from the hotbar slot.

Armor mitigation and armor durability still use the pre-existing Solaris armor
table/path and remain to be unified with item component armor facts in a later
M33.e slice.

## M33.d / M33.h Damage, Loot, And XP Notes

Fall damage now applies when the player lands after a fall of more than three
blocks. This is intentionally narrow and independent of vanilla's full movement
history model, but it gives M33 a non-melee damage source through the shared
survival health path.

Entity death continues to emit loot through repo/sidecar loot seams. The
repo-owned fallback loot table now includes common overworld logs dropping
themselves, fixing the survival tree-breaking gap where `oak_log` could be mined
but yielded no log item.

XP orbs now use server-owned entities with `experience_value`, spawn on common
mob death, synchronize via existing `AddEntity` / remove / take-item paths, and
update `ClientboundSetExperience` on pickup. Player XP level/progress/total are
already persisted in playerdata and now update during runtime pickup.

Chunk streaming no longer holds the shared `WorldStorage` mutex while generating
missing chunks. The stream path now loads the center chunk without invoking the
generator under lock, clones cached neighbours only for lighting context, and
generates/installs a missing center chunk across short critical sections. This
keeps survival block breaks from waiting behind background terrain generation.

## Deferred Gaps

Projectiles and status-effect packet sync are deferred. The local protocol dump
identifies `ClientboundRemoveMobEffectPacket`, `ClientboundUpdateMobEffectPacket`,
and `ClientboundSetExperiencePacket`, but M33 only added the fully needed and
tested XP packet. Effect sync still needs a mob-effect registry lookup seam and
packet implementation before gameplay effects can be claimed. Bow/skeleton
projectiles likewise still need server-owned projectile entity behavior and
verified hit/remove semantics.

Other remaining gaps: shields, enchantments, potions, explosions, fire/lava and
drowning runtime hooks, armor facts unified from item components, richer
knockback/line-of-sight, and vanilla parity differences in combat math.
